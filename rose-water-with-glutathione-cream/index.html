Rose water with glutathione cream: can you use both, and in what order?

Can you use rose water with glutathione cream? The right order, how long to wait, what to avoid mixing in, and a simple night routine for Indian skin.

Can I use rose water with glutathione cream?

Yes. Rose water is a light toner and does not stop glutathione cream from working. Splash or spray it on clean skin after face wash, let it dry for about a minute, then apply a pea-sized amount of glutathione cream. Choose plain rose water without alcohol or strong perfume.

Should I apply rose water before or after glutathione cream?

Before. Rose water is thin and watery, so it goes on first, straight after cleansing. The cream goes on last so it can sit on the skin overnight. Spraying rose water on top of the cream only dilutes it and can make it slide around.

Does rose water with glutathione cream make skin fair?

No. Neither changes your natural skin colour. Rose water is a refreshing toner. A glutathione cream, used with sunscreen every morning, can help skin look brighter and help tan and dark marks look lighter slowly, over 8 to 12 weeks.

The right order: rose water first, cream last

  1. Cleanse with a gentle face wash and lukewarm water. Gluta Boost Face Wash is made for this step.
  2. Pat dry with a clean towel, leaving the skin slightly damp.
  3. Rose water: spray it from about 20 cm away, or press it on with clean fingers or a cotton pad. Do not rub.
  4. Wait about a minute until the face no longer feels wet.
  5. Glutathione cream: a pea-sized amount, dotted on the forehead, cheeks and chin, then spread in gentle upward strokes. Keep it away from the eyes and lips.
  6. In the morning: rose water again if you like, a light moisturiser, and sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher.

How to choose a rose water

  • Read the label. Look for rose water or rose distillate high on the list.
  • Avoid alcohol high on the list and strong added perfume, especially on dry or sensitive skin.
  • Keep it clean. Close the cap, do not dip fingers into the bottle, and stop using it if the smell changes.
  • Patch test a new rose water on the jawline before using it on the whole face. Rose is a fragrance and some people react to it.

What not to mix with rose water and glutathione cream

  • Lemon juice, baking soda or toothpaste: these irritate skin and do not help tone. Never add them to rose water.
  • Glycerine on oily skin in humid weather: the old rose water and glycerine mix can feel sticky and heavy under a night cream. A thin layer of cream alone is enough.
  • Another strong active the same night: if your cream has retinol or salicylic acid, do not add a peel, scrub or extra acid toner. Read glutathione and salicylic acid.
  • Rose water sprayed on top of the cream: it waters the cream down. If your face feels dry later, add a plain moisturiser instead; see glutathione cream and moisturizer.

Rose water myths you may hear

  • "Rose water opens pores so the cream goes deeper." Pores do not open and close like doors. Rose water simply leaves the skin slightly damp, which helps a cream spread evenly.
  • "Rose water and glutathione together remove tan in a week." No toner or cream does that. Tan and dark marks can look lighter slowly, over weeks, and only with sunscreen every morning. See glutathione for dark spots and tan.
  • "More rose water means more glow." A few sprays are enough. Soaking the face just dilutes the cream that follows.
  • "Rose water can replace face wash at night." It cannot lift sunscreen, sweat and city dust. Cleanse first, then use rose water.
