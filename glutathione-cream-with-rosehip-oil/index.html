Glutathione cream with rosehip oil: the right order, how much, and what rosehip can and cannot do

Can I use rosehip oil with glutathione cream?

Yes, if your skin is dry or normal. Apply the glutathione cream first on clean, dry skin, wait a few minutes, then press one or two drops of pure rosehip oil over dry areas. Do not mix the oil into the jar, and use it at night rather than under sunscreen in hot weather.

Should rosehip oil go before or after cream?

After. Oils sit on top of the skin and slow water loss, so they work best as the last step of a night routine. Applying oil first makes the cream spread unevenly and feel greasy.

Does rosehip oil remove dark spots?

No. Rosehip oil is a nourishing oil that helps dry skin feel softer and smoother. It does not remove dark spots, tan or acne marks. Marks fade slowly over weeks, mainly with daily sunscreen and a consistent routine.

You can use rosehip oil with glutathione cream, as long as you layer them rather than mix them. Apply the cream first on clean, dry skin, wait a few minutes, then press one or two drops of pure rosehip oil over the dry parts of your face as the last step of your night routine.

Rosehip oil is pressed from the seeds of wild rose fruit. It is rich in essential fatty acids, feels light, and is popular in face oils for dry and rough-feeling skin. Online, it is often sold as a "scar and spot" oil. It can make dry skin feel softer and smoother, but it is not a brightening treatment and it does not change your skin colour.

The right order: cream first, rosehip oil last

  1. Wash your face with a gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash and pat dry.
  2. Wait until skin is fully dry, especially on nights you use a retinol cream.
  3. Apply a pea-sized amount of glutathione cream over the face and neck.
  4. Wait 5 to 10 minutes so the cream settles.
  5. Warm one or two drops of rosehip oil between your fingertips and press them over dry areas such as the cheeks, around the nose and the jawline. Skip an oily T-zone.
  6. Next morning: face wash, moisturiser if needed, and a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or more.

Two drops is plenty for most faces. For the full cream method, read how to use glutathione cream.

Fresh oil matters: storing rosehip in Indian heat

Rosehip oil is rich in delicate fatty acids, so it goes rancid faster than many other oils, especially in heat and light. Old oil smells sharp, like crayons or old nuts, and can irritate skin.

  • Buy a small bottle you will finish in a few months, not a large one.
  • Choose a dark glass bottle with a dropper and a use-by date.
  • Keep it cool and out of sunlight: not on a sunny windowsill or near the stove. In hot Indian summers, a cool cupboard is better than the bathroom shelf.
  • Smell it before use. If the smell has changed, throw it away.
  • Check the label: it should list rose hip seed oil only, without added fragrance.

Keep the cream itself cool and closed too; see how to store glutathione cream.

Rosehip vs other face oils

Rosehip feels lighter than coconut or castor oil but goes off sooner, while jojoba is very stable and suits combination skin. If you are choosing one oil for winter, see jojoba oil, almond oil and olive oil with glutathione cream. You only need one oil, not several.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for acne that is painful, cystic or leaves pitted scars, for brown patches on both cheeks that may be melasma, or if an oil causes itching, rash or swelling. Patch test any new oil on your jawline first.
