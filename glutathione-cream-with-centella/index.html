Glutathione cream with centella (cica): how to layer them and when a cica product helps

Can I use centella with glutathione cream?

Yes. Centella asiatica (cica) is a gentle plant extract that usually layers well. Apply a thin cica serum or toner first on clean, dry skin, then the glutathione cream on top, or use a cica moisturiser on the nights you skip a retinol cream. Patch test both first.

Should I apply cica before or after glutathione cream?

Go thinnest to thickest. A watery cica serum or toner goes before the cream; a thick cica balm or cream is better used on its own on a different night, or on dry patches after the cream. You do not need two heavy creams on the same night.

Does centella remove dark spots or tan?

No. Centella is used in skincare because it helps skin feel calm and comfortable. It does not remove tan, dark spots or pigmentation and does not change skin colour. Marks look lighter slowly over weeks, mainly with daily sunscreen.

Centella asiatica, called gotu kola in English and mandukaparni in Ayurveda, is a small leafy plant. In skincare it shows up as "cica" in serums, toners, sheet masks and balms, made popular by Korean skincare. People use it because it helps skin feel calm and comfortable, especially when it feels tight or a little irritated. GlutaNex Night Cream does not contain centella, so this guide is about using a separate cica product alongside it.

The right order: thin to thick

  1. Wash your face with a gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash and pat dry.
  2. Wait until skin is fully dry on nights you use a retinol cream. Damp skin can make retinol sting more.
  3. Cica toner or serum (optional): two or three drops, pressed in. Let it settle for a minute or two.
  4. Glutathione cream: a pea-sized amount over the face and neck.
  5. Thick cica balm (only if needed): a thin layer on dry patches such as the sides of the nose or around the mouth, after the cream.
  6. Next morning: face wash, a light moisturiser if needed, and a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or more.

Read the label: choose a product without strong fragrance, and check that "Centella asiatica extract", "madecassoside" or "asiaticoside" is actually in the ingredient list, not just in the name.

Who it suits

  • Sensitive skin: a simple cica serum or moisturiser can help skin feel more comfortable while it gets used to an active cream. Read glutathione cream for sensitive skin.
  • Oily and combination skin: watery cica toners and serums sit well in humid weather without heaviness. See glutathione for oily skin.
  • Dry skin: a cica toner alone is not enough; you still need a cream. A cica balm on dry patches can help in winter.
  • Everyone: patch test the cica product on the jawline for 3 nights before the whole face. A plant extract can still irritate some people.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for acne that is painful or cystic, a rash that spreads or keeps coming back, eczema, or brown patches on both cheeks that may be melasma. Cica products are for comfort, not for treating skin conditions.
