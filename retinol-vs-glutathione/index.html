Retinol vs glutathione: which is better for your skin?

What each one does, how they compare, and how to use both safely.

By the GlutaNex team · Updated 11 October 2026 · 2 sources cited

Short answer: retinol and glutathione do different jobs. Retinol has stronger evidence for smoother texture, fine lines and acne marks, but it can irritate. Glutathione is a gentler antioxidant used for brighter, more even-looking tone. Many routines use both.

Retinol (vitamin A)Glutathione
Main useTexture, fine lines, acne marks, clogged poresBrighter, more even-looking tone; antioxidant support
How it worksSpeeds up skin cell turnoverSlows melanin production and neutralises free radicals
EvidenceStrong, decades of research on retinoidsPromising but small studies (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025)
IrritationCommon at first: dryness, peeling, rednessGenerally gentle
When to useNight onlyNight or day, depending on the product
PregnancyAvoid unless your doctor approvesAsk your doctor

Which should you choose?

  • Mainly dull skin, tan or uneven tone: start with glutathione.
  • Mainly texture, fine lines or acne marks: retinol is the stronger option.
  • Both: a routine with both, introduced slowly.

Using retinol and glutathione together

  1. Use them at night, on clean dry skin.
  2. Start 2 to 3 nights a week for two weeks, then every other night, then nightly if comfortable.
  3. Moisturise if skin feels dry.
  4. Sunscreen every morning: retinol makes skin more sun-sensitive.
  5. Do not add another retinol or exfoliating acid product on the same night.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use retinol and glutathione together?

Yes. Use them at night, start 2 to 3 nights a week, and use sunscreen every morning. Do not add another retinol product on the same night.

Is glutathione gentler than retinol?

Generally yes. Retinol often causes dryness and peeling at first, while glutathione is usually well tolerated.
