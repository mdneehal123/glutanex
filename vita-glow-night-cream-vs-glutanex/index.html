Can a night cream make skin fair in 7 days?

No. Skin renews over weeks, and tan and dark marks usually take 8 to 12 weeks of nightly care and daily sunscreen to look lighter. Treat any promise of fairness in a few days as a warning sign, not a benefit.

How to check any night cream before you use it

  1. Full ingredient list printed on the box or jar. "Herbal" or "glutathione formula" alone is not a list.
  2. Maker or marketer name and full address, plus batch number, manufacturing date and expiry date.
  3. Sensible claims. Be wary of "fair in 7 days", "100% safe", "no side effects" or "permanent whitening".
  4. Price that makes sense. A branded cream sold far below its usual price by an unknown seller may not be genuine.
  5. Patch test on the jaw or inner arm for 24 to 48 hours.

Why very fast whitening is a warning sign

Health regulators in Singapore, Malaysia and Hong Kong have repeatedly found whitening night creams sold online with undeclared mercury, strong steroids, hydroquinone or tretinoin. These can give fast results at first, then cause thinning, redness, rashes or worse. This is a general warning about unlabelled creams, not a finding about any brand on this page. Read glutathione side effects and glutathione vs hydroquinone to understand the difference.

Switching from another night cream

  • If your current cream gave very fast results, or your skin burns, thins or turns red when you stop it, see a dermatologist before switching. Stopping a cream with a hidden steroid can cause a flare.
  • Otherwise, stop the old cream, give your skin 3 to 5 nights with a plain moisturiser, then start GlutaNex 2 nights a week.
  • Do not layer two whitening or brightening night creams on the same night.
