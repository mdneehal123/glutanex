Glutathione cream for 40 plus: what it can do for mature skin

How skin changes after 40, what retinol, peptides and glutathione can do for the look of fine lines and age spots, and when to see a dermatologist.

Is glutathione cream good for skin over 40?

It can be a useful part of a routine. Glutathione is an antioxidant, and a cream that also contains retinol, peptides and moisturisers can help mature skin look smoother, brighter and more even over a few months. Daily sunscreen still does more for age spots than any cream.

Can glutathione reduce age spots?

It may help sun-related age spots look a little lighter over 8 to 12 weeks, with daily sunscreen, but changes in studies were modest. Any spot that is new, growing, irregular, rough or bleeding should be checked by a dermatologist before you try any cream.

Does glutathione help with wrinkles?

Evidence is limited. One 12-week study of oral glutathione found some improvement in wrinkles with the reduced form, but most results were not statistically significant. For the look of fine lines, retinol and sunscreen have a stronger track record; glutathione is a supporting antioxidant.

How skin changes after 40

  • Slower renewal: skin sheds dead cells more slowly, so it looks duller and rougher.
  • Less collagen: fine lines around the eyes and mouth deepen, and skin feels less firm.
  • Drier skin: oil production drops, and after menopause falling oestrogen makes skin noticeably drier and thinner.
  • Age spots and uneven tone: decades of sun show up as brown spots on the cheeks, temples, hands and forearms.
  • Slower healing: marks from acne, threading or minor injuries take longer to fade.

None of this needs "fixing", but a few well-chosen ingredients and steady sun protection can help skin look smoother, more even and more comfortable.

What the research says

A 10-week study of a 2% glutathione lotion on the face found a modest fall in melanin and improvements in moisture and smoothness (Watanabe et al., 2014). A 12-week study of oral glutathione in women aged 20 to 50 found some improvement in wrinkles with the reduced form and a trend towards better elasticity, though most results were not statistically significant (Weschawalit et al., 2017). A 2025 review concluded that topical glutathione looks safe and modestly helpful, but studies are small (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025). In short: helpful as part of a routine, not a replacement for retinol and sunscreen. Our benefits for skin page has the details.

A simple routine for 40 plus

  1. Morning: rinse or gentle cleanse, moisturiser, broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen on face, neck and the backs of the hands.
  2. Night: gentle cleanse with a mild face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash, pat dry, a pea-sized amount of night cream on the face and neck, then a moisturiser if your skin feels dry.
  3. Weeks 1–2: night cream 2 to 3 nights a week. Plain moisturiser on other nights.
  4. Weeks 3–6: increase slowly if your skin is comfortable. Many mature-skin users settle on alternate nights.
  5. Weekly: no harsh scrubs. Retinol already does the exfoliating.

Give it 8 to 12 weeks before judging. See how long it takes to work.

Menopause and dry skin

Around menopause many women find their skin suddenly drier, itchier and more reactive, and products that worked for years start to sting. If this is you:

  • Use the moisturiser sandwich: a thin layer of moisturiser, then the night cream, then moisturiser again.
  • Use a cream cleanser or just water in the morning.
  • Choose cream sunscreens over gels or mattifying formulas.
  • Cut the night cream back to two nights a week in winter if needed.

More tips are in our dry skin guide. If dryness, flushing or itching is severe, talk to your doctor; hormonal changes can affect the whole body, not just the skin.

Age spots: when to see a dermatologist

Most brown spots after 40 are harmless sun spots. But some spots need a doctor's look before you put any brightening cream on them. See a dermatologist if a spot:

  • is new and growing, or has changed shape, size or colour;
  • has an irregular border or more than one colour;
  • is raised, rough, scaly, itchy or bleeds;
  • does not heal;
  • appears as patchy brown areas on both cheeks, which may be melasma (see glutathione for melasma).

A cream can make harmless sun spots look a little lighter over months. It cannot tell you whether a spot is harmless. That is a dermatologist's job.

Do not forget the neck and hands

After 40, the neck and the backs of the hands often show sun damage as clearly as the face, and they are the areas most often missed. Carry your sunscreen down the front and sides of the neck every morning and onto the backs of the hands, reapplying on the hands after washing if you are going out. At night, the night cream can be carried down the neck after the face; use less there, because neck skin is thinner, and add a moisturiser on top.

For the hands, a plain moisturiser after every wash and sunscreen in the day do most of the work; a 30 g face cream is sized for the face and neck rather than the hands. Read more in hands and feet tan and dark neck.

Men over 40 can follow the same routine. Shaving already exfoliates the beard area, so apply the night cream there with extra care and avoid it on freshly shaved, irritated skin.

What to skip

Stacking several strong actives at once, very hot water on the face, harsh scrubs and any cream that promises to remove wrinkles or spots in days. Mature skin is slower to recover from irritation, and irritation makes uneven tone look worse. Introduce one new product at a time, give each one a few weeks, and keep a plain moisturiser on hand for nights when your skin feels tired or tight. Consistency over months matters far more than a long shelf of products.
