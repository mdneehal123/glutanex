Glutathione cream with malai: how to use a malai face pack and your cream on the same night, safely

Can I use malai with glutathione cream?

Yes, but not mixed together. Use fresh malai as a short rinse-off pack for about 10 minutes, wash it off with a gentle face wash, let your face dry fully, then apply the glutathione cream. Keep malai for dry or normal skin, and patch test first.

Should I apply malai before or after glutathione cream?

Before, and always rinsed off. Malai left on overnight over a cream can turn sour, smell, and clog pores, especially in Indian heat. Pack first, wash, dry, then cream as the last step.

Does malai remove tan or make skin fair?

No. Malai can make dry skin feel softer for a while after you rinse it off, but it does not remove tan or dark spots and does not change skin colour. Tan and marks look lighter slowly over weeks, mainly with daily sunscreen.

You can use malai with glutathione cream, as long as the malai is a short rinse-off pack and the cream goes on afterwards on clean, dry skin. Never mix malai into the cream jar or leave it on overnight: it is a fresh dairy food, and it spoils fast at room temperature.

Malai, the thick layer of cream that forms on top of boiled milk, is one of the oldest home face packs in Indian kitchens, often with a pinch of haldi or besan. It is rich in milk fat, so it can make dry, rough skin feel softer for a while. It is not a brightening treatment, and on oily or acne-prone skin it can feel heavy.

The right order on a malai night

  1. Take fresh malai from milk boiled the same day, about one teaspoon. Use a clean spoon, not fingers dipped into the bowl.
  2. Patch test first: a little on the jawline for 10 minutes on one night, then wait a day.
  3. Apply a thin layer on a clean face, avoiding the eye area, and leave it for about 10 minutes. Do not let it dry and crack.
  4. Rinse with lukewarm water, then wash with a gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash to remove the fat and milk smell.
  5. Pat dry and wait until your face is fully dry, especially before a retinol cream. Damp skin can make retinol sting more.
  6. Apply a pea-sized amount of glutathione cream over the face and neck as the last step.
  7. Next morning: face wash, a light moisturiser if needed, and a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or more.

Once or twice a week is plenty. For the full cream method, read how to use glutathione cream.

Keep it fresh: malai hygiene in Indian weather

  • Use it the same day. Malai left out in summer can turn sour within hours. If it smells sour or looks yellow and watery, throw it away.
  • Keep the bowl in the fridge and take out only what you need with a clean spoon.
  • Never put malai, or any kitchen ingredient, into the cream jar. It brings water and germs into the jar and can spoil the cream; see how to store glutathione cream.
  • Skip it if you have a milk allergy or if dairy on skin has itched before.

Malai with haldi, besan or honey

Many families add a pinch of haldi, a spoon of besan or a few drops of honey to malai. Keep the pack simple and gentle:

  • Haldi: a small pinch only; too much can leave a yellow tint. Read glutathione and turmeric.
  • Besan: makes a thicker pack. Wash it off gently and do not scrub, especially on retinol weeks. See besan with glutathione cream.
  • Honey: sticky but mild; see honey with glutathione cream.
  • Do not add lemon. Lemon juice can sting and irritate, more so with a retinol cream; see why to skip lemon.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for acne that is painful or cystic, brown patches on both cheeks that may be melasma, or if any home pack causes itching, rash or swelling. Kitchen packs are for comfort, not for skin conditions.
