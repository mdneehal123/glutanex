Is glutathione safe in pregnancy?

What is known about glutathione, retinol and skincare during pregnancy and breastfeeding.

By the GlutaNex team · Updated 11 October 2026 · 2 sources cited

Key takeaways
  • Glutathione safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding has not been well studied.
  • Avoid retinol creams and glutathione injections; ask your doctor before any glutathione product.
  • Gentle cleanser, moisturiser and daily sunscreen are safe basics.

Short answer: the safety of glutathione during pregnancy and breastfeeding has not been well studied, so speak to your doctor before using any glutathione product. Avoid glutathione injections and avoid creams containing retinol.

Creams

Little glutathione is absorbed from a cream, but many glutathione creams also contain other actives. Retinol (vitamin A) is generally avoided in pregnancy. The GlutaNex Night Cream contains retinol and salicylic acid, so do not use it while pregnant or breastfeeding unless your doctor says it is fine.

Tablets and injections

There are no good safety studies of glutathione supplements in pregnancy. Injections carry extra risks and warnings (US FDA warning, 2015); avoid them.

Pregnancy-friendly basics

  • A gentle cleanser and a simple moisturiser.
  • Sunscreen every day; pregnancy can make skin more prone to dark patches (melasma).
  • Ask your doctor or dermatologist before adding any active ingredient.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use glutathione cream during pregnancy?

Ask your doctor first. Avoid creams with retinol, such as the GlutaNex Night Cream, while pregnant or breastfeeding.

Are glutathione tablets safe in pregnancy?

They have not been studied well in pregnancy. Do not take them without your doctor's advice.
