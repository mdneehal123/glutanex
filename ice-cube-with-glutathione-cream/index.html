Ice cube on face with glutathione cream: right order and safe use

Can you use an ice cube on your face with glutathione cream? Yes: wrap the ice in a cloth, keep it moving for about a minute, let skin warm back up, then apply the cream. What ice can and cannot do, and who should skip it.

Can I use an ice cube on my face with glutathione cream?

Yes. Wrap an ice cube in a clean, thin cloth, glide it over clean skin for about a minute without stopping in one place, then pat dry and wait a few minutes for your skin to warm back up before applying a thin layer of glutathione cream. Never freeze the cream into ice cubes.

Should I apply ice before or after glutathione cream?

Before. Ice goes on clean skin as a short cooling step; the cream goes on afterwards once the skin is dry and back to normal temperature. Rubbing ice over the cream only wets and wipes it away.

Does ice cube with glutathione cream make skin fair or shrink pores?

No. Ice can make skin look less puffy and feel tighter for a short while, and a glutathione cream with daily sunscreen may help tan and dark marks look lighter over 8 to 12 weeks. Neither changes your natural skin colour or permanently shrinks pores.

The right order on a cream night

  1. Wash your face with a gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash and pat dry.
  2. Wrap one ice cube in a clean, thin cotton cloth or handkerchief. Do not use bare ice straight from the tray.
  3. Glide it gently over the forehead, cheeks, nose and chin for about a minute in total. Keep it moving and never hold it on one spot for more than a few seconds.
  4. Pat the face dry with a clean towel.
  5. Wait 3 to 5 minutes until your skin feels dry and back to normal temperature.
  6. Apply a thin, pea-sized layer of glutathione cream on the face, avoiding the lips and the skin right at the lash line.
  7. Sunscreen every morning, SPF 30 or higher.

What ice can and cannot do

Cold briefly narrows small blood vessels near the surface, so skin can look less red and puffy and feel tighter for a short time. That is why ice feels good on tired morning eyes or after a hot commute. The effect fades as the skin warms up. Ice does not close or shrink pores, does not remove tan or dark spots, and does not make a cream absorb more deeply. Pores are shaped by oil, genes and age; our glutathione for open pores guide explains what helps them look smaller over time.

Ice in the morning: puffiness and makeup

Many people use ice in the morning, before makeup. That is fine, but remember the GlutaNex Night Cream is for night only. A good morning order is: gentle wash, wrapped ice for a minute, pat dry, a light moisturiser, sunscreen, then makeup. For under-eye puffiness, a cold spoon or a cool damp cloth works much the same as ice and is gentler on thin skin. Dark circles are a different matter; see glutathione for dark circles, and for makeup timing see makeup after glutathione cream.

Who should skip ice on the face

  • Very dry or winter-cracked skin, especially in cold North Indian winters; see glutathione cream in winter.
  • Sensitive or easily red skin, or broken veins on the cheeks; see glutathione cream for sensitive skin.
  • Anyone whose fingers turn white or blue in the cold, or who has a condition that makes them very sensitive to cold. Ask your doctor first.
  • Open cuts, picked pimples or freshly waxed skin.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if your skin stays red, blistered, numb or painful after ice, if facial redness and flushing keep coming back, or if puffiness around the eyes lasts for days. These need a doctor rather than a home remedy.
