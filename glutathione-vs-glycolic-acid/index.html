Glutathione vs glycolic acid: which is better?

In the glutathione vs glycolic acid question, they do different jobs. Glycolic acid is an AHA exfoliant that removes dull surface cells; glutathione is an antioxidant used for a more even-looking tone over weeks. Glycolic acts faster on texture but irritates more. Neither is simply better; it depends on your skin and the rest of your routine.

Can I use glycolic acid and glutathione cream together?

Not on the same night if your glutathione cream contains retinol or salicylic acid, as GlutaNex Night Cream does. That stacks three strong actives and often causes redness and peeling. If your skin tolerates both, use them on separate nights, with sunscreen every morning.

Glycolic acid's speed is real but comes with more risk. Glutathione's effect is gentler and also modest: a 2025 systematic review called topical results modest (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025). If you want the ingredient side explained, see the skincare glossary.

Why you should not add glycolic acid on the same night

GlutaNex Glutathione Night Cream already contains two exfoliating or resurfacing actives: retinol (vitamin A) and salicylic acid (a BHA). Adding a glycolic toner, serum or peel underneath means:

  • an AHA, a BHA and a retinoid on the same skin in one night;
  • a much higher chance of burning, redness, raw patches and peeling;
  • a weakened skin barrier, which makes dullness and uneven tone look worse;
  • on brown Indian skin, a real risk of irritation turning into dark marks (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation).

This is also why our usage instructions say not to use the cream on the same night as other retinol, AHA/BHA or peel products. If you already feel stinging, read glutathione cream burning.

Do you need glycolic acid at all?

For many people, no. The salicylic acid and retinol in the night cream already give gentle exfoliation and smoother texture over time. Adding glycolic acid makes sense mainly if:

  • you have used both products before without irritation;
  • your skin is oily or rough rather than dry or sensitive;
  • you are happy to keep a strict schedule and wear sunscreen daily.

An alternate-night plan (only if your skin tolerates it)

Settle into the night cream first, for at least 3–4 weeks with no irritation. Then, if you still want glycolic acid:

NightWhat to use
MonGlutathione night cream
TueRest night: cleanser and plain moisturiser
WedGlutathione night cream
ThuGlycolic acid product (low strength), then moisturiser
FriRest night
SatGlutathione night cream
SunRest night

Start with glycolic once a week, never the night before or after a waxing, threading or facial session. Rest nights are not wasted; they let the barrier recover.

A few rules make this safer. Choose a low-strength, leave-on glycolic product rather than a strong at-home peel. Apply it on clean, fully dry skin, and keep it away from the eyes, the corners of the nose and the lips. Do not use it on broken skin, active pimples you have picked, sunburn, or skin that is already peeling from the night cream. If you feel more than a brief, mild tingle, rinse it off with cool water. And if your skin reacts during the first month, drop the glycolic product completely: the night cream on its own, used steadily with sunscreen, is the routine that matters. Changes in tan and tone take 8–12 weeks either way; see how long glutathione cream takes to work.

For most people starting out, the order is simple: night cream and sunscreen first, wait two months, then decide whether anything else is worth adding.

Signs you have overdone exfoliation

  • Skin looks shiny but feels tight, like cling film.
  • Products that never stung before now sting, even a plain moisturiser.
  • Redness, raw patches or peeling around the nose, mouth or chin.
  • New dark patches where skin was irritated.

If you notice these, stop the glycolic product and the night cream for a few nights. Use only a gentle cleanser, a plain moisturiser and sunscreen until skin feels normal, then restart the night cream 2–3 nights a week. If redness or burning does not settle within a few days, see a doctor. More on reactions in glutathione side effects.

Sunscreen is not optional

Glycolic acid, retinol and salicylic acid all make skin more sun-sensitive. Without daily sunscreen, any brightening gain is lost to fresh tan, and exfoliated skin can darken faster. Use a broad-spectrum SPF 30+ with a good PA rating every morning and reapply when outdoors for long; see glutathione cream and sunscreen.

Sensitive skin, pregnancy and medical concerns

  • Sensitive or rosacea-prone skin: skip glycolic acid. Start the night cream slowly instead; see glutathione cream for sensitive skin.
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: GlutaNex Night Cream contains retinol, so do not use it unless your doctor says it is fine. Ask your doctor about any acid product too; see glutathione in pregnancy.
  • Melasma or stubborn patches: clinic glycolic peels are a medical procedure. Speak to a dermatologist rather than combining strong products at home.
