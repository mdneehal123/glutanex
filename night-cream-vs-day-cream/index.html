What is the difference between day cream and night cream?

A day cream is light, protects against sun and pollution, and often contains SPF. A night cream has no SPF and is usually richer, with repair and brightening actives such as retinol, niacinamide or glutathione that work best without sun exposure.

Can I use night cream in the morning?

Only if it has no retinol or exfoliating acids, and always under sunscreen. Night creams with retinol or salicylic acid, such as GlutaNex Night Cream, should be used at night only.

Do I need both a day cream and a night cream?

Not necessarily. Many people only need a light moisturiser with sunscreen in the morning and one good night cream. What you cannot skip is sunscreen in the day.

The simple difference: a day cream protects, and a night cream repairs and brightens. Day creams are light and often have SPF. Night creams have no SPF, may be richer, and carry the active ingredients that work best while you sleep, away from the sun.

Why some actives must stay at night

  • Retinol breaks down in sunlight and makes skin more sun-sensitive. See retinol vs glutathione.
  • Salicylic and other acids loosen surface skin, so the new skin underneath burns and tans more easily. See glutathione and salicylic acid.
  • Rich textures feel greasy in humid Indian afternoons and pick up dust and pollution.

Glutathione itself can be used morning or night; whether your cream is day or night depends on its other ingredients. See glutathione cream: morning or night?

A simple routine for Indian weather

Morning: gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash, light moisturiser if you need it, sunscreen SPF 30+. In humid summer, a gel moisturiser or just sunscreen may be enough. See glutathione cream with sunscreen.

Night: remove sunscreen and makeup, wash, pat dry, apply a pea-sized night cream such as GlutaNex Glutathione Night Cream. In dry winter, add a plain moisturiser on top. See glutathione cream and moisturizer and glutathione cream in winter.

Who can skip the day cream

  • Oily skin in summer: a moisturising sunscreen is often enough.
  • Normal skin: sunscreen plus a light gel.
  • Dry skin and winter: keep a moisturiser under sunscreen.

Nobody should skip sunscreen, especially when using a night cream with retinol or acids.
