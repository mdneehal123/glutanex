Glutathione cream for combination skin: oily T-zone, dry cheeks

How to use a glutathione cream when your T-zone is oily and your cheeks are dry, with amounts, humid-weather tips and a weekly routine.

Is glutathione cream good for combination skin?

Yes, a glutathione cream for combination skin works well if it is balanced rather than heavy. Ingredients like niacinamide and salicylic acid help an oily T-zone, while hyaluronic acid and ceramides comfort dry cheeks. Use a pea-sized amount, spread it thinner on the T-zone, and wear sunscreen every morning.

How do I apply cream on combination skin?

Start with a pea-sized amount. Dot it on the forehead, nose, chin and both cheeks, then spread a thin layer over the oily T-zone and a little more on the drier cheeks and jawline. If cheeks still feel tight, add a light moisturiser only there.

What combination skin actually means

Combination skin is oily in the T-zone (forehead, nose and chin) and normal to dry on the cheeks and jawline. Pores on the nose often look bigger, the forehead shines by afternoon, and yet the cheeks can feel tight after washing. It changes with the seasons too: in the Indian monsoon the whole face may feel oily, while in a Delhi or Pune winter the cheeks dry out fast.

That is why one heavy cream applied the same way everywhere rarely suits it. The trick is the same product, applied differently by zone.

Glutathione cream for combination skin: how much and where

  1. Cleanse gently at night. A mild face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash removes oil and sunscreen without stripping the cheeks. Pat dry.
  2. Take a pea-sized amount. That is enough for the whole face; more does not work faster.
  3. Dot it on five points: forehead, nose, chin, and each cheek.
  4. Spread thin on the T-zone, using what is left on your fingertips for the nose.
  5. Spread a little more on cheeks and jawline, and down onto the neck.
  6. Fix only the dry areas. If cheeks still feel tight after a few minutes, add a light moisturiser there, not on the forehead.

Keep the cream away from the eyes, the corners of the nose and the lips, which are thin and sting easily.

How niacinamide and salicylic acid help the T-zone

Combination skin benefits from ingredients that calm oil without drying the rest of the face:

  • Niacinamide (vitamin B3) helps the look of oiliness and enlarged pores and supports the barrier, so it suits both zones. See glutathione and niacinamide.
  • Salicylic acid (BHA) is oil-soluble, so it works inside oily pores on the nose and chin to help skin feel smoother and less congested.
  • Hyaluronic acid and ceramides look after the drier cheeks.

Tips for humid Indian weather

  • Choose a gel or fluid sunscreen with a good PA rating; thick creams feel greasy on the forehead. Reapply when outdoors for long. See sunscreen tips.
  • Do not over-wash. Washing three or four times a day leaves cheeks dry and can make the T-zone produce more oil. Twice a day is enough; rinse with water after heavy sweat.
  • Blot, do not powder heavily. Blotting paper takes oil off without clogging pores.
  • Skip heavy oils and butters on the T-zone in summer and monsoon; keep any richer layer for the cheeks.
  • Mind AC rooms. Office AC dries cheeks even in humid cities; a light moisturiser on the cheeks in the afternoon helps.

Pimples on the T-zone

Combination skin often gets small bumps and blackheads on the nose, chin and forehead. The salicylic acid in the night cream helps keep pores clearer over time, but give it a few weeks. Meanwhile, do not squeeze or pick: on Indian skin a squeezed pimple often leaves a dark mark that lasts longer than the pimple itself. Change pillowcases often, wipe your phone screen, and keep hair oil off the forehead, as heavy oils left overnight can clog pores along the hairline. If pimples are painful, deep or leave scars, see a dermatologist rather than adding more products. For marks that are already there, read glutathione for acne marks.

Common mistakes

  • Using drying acne products all over the face, which leaves cheeks raw.
  • Applying a thick layer of night cream on the nose, then getting bumps or shine.
  • Adding several new actives (AHA toners, peels, a second retinol) at the same time; see glutathione vs glycolic acid.
  • Judging too early. A 2025 systematic review described topical glutathione's effect as modest (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025), and tan or marks take 8–12 weeks with daily sunscreen. The cream will not change your natural skin colour.

If you get tingling or dryness on the cheeks in the first weeks, go back to 2–3 nights a week. Persistent redness or burning means stop; read glutathione cream burning.
