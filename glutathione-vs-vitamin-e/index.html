Glutathione vs vitamin E for skin: differences and using them together

Two antioxidants that do different jobs on the skin: what each one does, who it suits, the vitamin E capsule habit, and how to combine them.

Glutathione vs vitamin E: which is better for skin?

In the glutathione vs vitamin E question, neither is simply better: they do different jobs. Vitamin E is an oil-soluble antioxidant that softens and moisturises dry skin and is often paired with vitamin C. Glutathione is used for a brighter, more even-looking tone. Many routines can use both, with daily sunscreen.

Can I use vitamin E with glutathione cream?

Yes, for most people. They are both antioxidants and do not clash. Apply the glutathione night cream first and, if your skin is dry, a vitamin E moisturiser on top or on alternate nights. Oily or acne-prone skin may find pure vitamin E oil too heavy.

This page compares the two as skincare ingredients you apply to the skin. Vitamin E and glutathione are also sold as supplements, but whether you should take either by mouth, and how much, is a question for your doctor, not a skincare page. We give no dosing advice here.

What vitamin E does on the skin

Vitamin E sits in the oily layer of the skin, where it helps protect the skin's own fats from damage by sun and pollution. In products it does three practical things:

  • Moisturises and softens. As an oil-soluble ingredient it helps dry, rough patches feel smoother. This is why it is common in winter creams and lip balms.
  • Works well with vitamin C. Vitamin C and vitamin E are often combined in day serums because each helps the other stay active against free radicals. Read glutathione vs vitamin C for how vitamin C fits in.
  • Protects the formula. Small amounts are added to many creams simply to stop the oils in them going rancid.

What vitamin E is not known for is fading tan or dark marks. It supports comfort and protection rather than tone.

What glutathione does on the skin

Glutathione is used in skincare for a brighter, more even-looking tone. A 10-week placebo-controlled study of a glutathione lotion found a modest fall in melanin index, with better moisture and smoothness (Watanabe et al., 2014), and a 2025 review called topical glutathione promising but backed by small, short studies (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025). It does not change natural skin colour (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016). Inside the body, glutathione also helps recycle vitamin E and vitamin C back into their active forms, which is one reason the three are often mentioned together. The vitamin E capsule habit

Many people in India break open a vitamin E capsule and rub the oil on their face. A few honest points:

  • Dry skin and dry patches: a drop mixed into your moisturiser can feel comforting, especially in north Indian winters.
  • Oily or acne-prone skin: pure vitamin E oil is thick and can feel greasy in humid weather and may make breakouts worse. A light gel moisturiser is usually a better choice. See glutathione for oily skin.
  • Allergy is possible. Some people get itchy, red rashes from vitamin E on the skin. Patch test on the jawline for 24 hours first.
  • Capsules are made to be swallowed. Using one on the skin is fine for most people, but whether to take vitamin E capsules by mouth, and how many, is for your doctor to decide.
  • Not on broken skin or fresh cuts, and keep it out of your eyes.

How to use glutathione and vitamin E together

  1. Morning: gentle cleanse, a vitamin C serum with vitamin E if you use one, moisturiser, then sunscreen SPF 30 or higher.
  2. Night: cleanse with a mild face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash, pat dry, then a pea-sized amount of a glutathione night cream.
  3. Dry skin: once the night cream has settled for a few minutes, add a vitamin E moisturiser or a drop of vitamin E mixed into a plain cream on top.
  4. Retinol nights: because the GlutaNex Night Cream contains retinol and salicylic acid, start it 2 to 3 nights a week. On the other nights, a vitamin E moisturiser alone is a good rest night.

Common mistakes with vitamin E and glutathione

  • Expecting vitamin E to fade tan. It makes skin feel softer and better protected, but it is not a brightening ingredient. If tan is your main worry, sunscreen and a leave-on tone product matter more.
  • Using pure vitamin E oil on oily skin in summer. In Mumbai, Chennai or Kolkata humidity, a thick oil on an already oily face often feels greasy and can clog pores. Save it for dry patches, elbows or winter.
  • Mixing many oils and actives in one go. Vitamin E capsule, almond oil, a lemon pack and a retinol cream in the same week makes it impossible to tell what is causing redness. Add one thing at a time and wait two weeks.
  • Skipping sunscreen because a product "has antioxidants". Antioxidants in a cream or serum support sunscreen; they do not replace it. Neither vitamin E nor glutathione has an SPF.
  • Putting either on the lips with a retinol cream. A plain balm is better for lips; keep retinol and acid creams off them.

A simple test of whether a combination suits you: after two weeks, does your skin feel comfortable, not tight, oily or itchy? If yes, keep it. If not, drop the newest product first.
