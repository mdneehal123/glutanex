Glutathione for pigmentation: types, what helps and what to avoid

The five common kinds of pigmentation on Indian skin, which ones a glutathione cream may help, and when to see a dermatologist.

By the GlutaNex team · Updated 14 October 2026 · 3 sources cited

Key takeaways
  • Tan and acne marks respond best to a glutathione cream used with daily sunscreen.
  • Melasma and changing spots need a dermatologist.
  • Give it 8–12 weeks; avoid steroid "fairness" creams.

Does glutathione reduce pigmentation?

It may help some types look lighter over 8 to 12 weeks, mainly tan and acne marks, when used with daily sunscreen. It works modestly and gradually, and it is not proven for melasma.

Which glutathione product is best for pigmentation?

A leave-on cream or serum, used at night, with SPF 30+ sunscreen every morning. Leave-on products are what the research tested; soaps and face washes rinse off too quickly.

A routine that helps most pigmentation

  1. Night: gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash, then a leave-on glutathione cream such as GlutaNex Glutathione Night Cream.
  2. Morning: broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen, reapplied outdoors.
  3. Hands off: do not pick pimples or scrub hard; irritation makes new marks.
  4. Wait: 8 to 12 weeks before judging. See how long glutathione takes to work.

What the research shows

In the best-known study, a 2% glutathione lotion lowered the melanin index more than placebo over 10 weeks (Watanabe et al., 2014). A 2025 review called topical glutathione promising but noted that studies are few and small (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025).

What to avoid

  • Steroid or unlabelled "fairness" creams that promise results in days (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016).
  • Lemon, baking soda or toothpaste on the face.
  • Starting several strong actives at once.

When to see a dermatologist

Patches on both cheeks, a spot that changes size, shape or colour, bleeding, or no change after 3 months of care.
