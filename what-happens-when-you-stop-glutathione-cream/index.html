What happens when you stop using glutathione cream?

What changes after you stop a glutathione cream, why there is no dramatic rebound, how to keep results, and when to pause.

What happens when you stop using glutathione?

When you stop using glutathione cream, nothing dramatic happens. Skin does not suddenly turn darker or rebound. Any brighter, more even look fades gradually over weeks, mainly if sun protection lapses and new tan builds up. Keeping daily sunscreen helps hold on to the improvement much longer.

Will my skin get darker if I stop glutathione cream?

Your skin will not become darker than it was before you started. Your natural colour stays the same throughout. What can come back is tan and new marks from sun, heat and friction, which is why sunscreen matters more than the cream.

This page is about glutathione creams and other skincare. Stopping glutathione tablets or injections is a different, medical question; ask the doctor who advised them, and read glutathione cream vs tablets vs injections for why injections are not recommended for cosmetic use.

The timing is a rough guide. Indian summers, outdoor work and two-wheeler commutes speed up the return of tan; staying mostly indoors slows it.

Why there is no "rebound"

Some people worry that stopping will make skin darker than before. That fear usually comes from two very different things:

  • Illegal "fairness" creams with steroids. Unlabelled creams containing steroids can cause redness, thinning and a flare when stopped. That is a steroid problem, not a glutathione one, and those creams should be stopped with a doctor's help.
  • Injections and drips. Claims about what happens after stopping these come from an unregulated area with no clinical trials for skin lightening (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016).

A cosmetic glutathione cream does not change your natural skin colour in the first place (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016), so there is nothing to rebound to. The changes seen in the 10-week lotion study were modest (Watanabe et al., 2014), and a 2025 review notes that studies were small and short and did not follow people long after stopping (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025). Honestly, we do not have data on exactly how long results last; the gradual fade above is what you would expect from how tan and pigment work.

What fades, and what stays

  • Tan: comes back with sun, whether or not you use a cream. Tan is the skin's response to UV, so sun habits decide it.
  • Dullness: partly from the cream's moisturisers and gentle exfoliation (retinol and salicylic acid in GlutaNex Glutathione Night Cream). Smoothness slowly returns to your usual within weeks of stopping.
  • Faded acne marks and dark spots: a mark that has faded stays faded. New pimples can leave new marks. See glutathione for acne marks.
  • Your natural skin colour: unchanged before, during and after.

How to keep your results after stopping

  1. Keep sunscreen every morning. SPF 30 or higher on face, neck and ears; reapply if you are outdoors. This single step decides most of what happens next. See glutathione cream and sunscreen.
  2. Keep a gentle cleanser and a moisturiser. A mild face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash and a plain night moisturiser keep the barrier healthy.
  3. Physical protection. A cap or dupatta and sunglasses on long rides, and a scarf for the neck.
  4. Do not pick spots. Picked pimples leave marks that last far longer than squeezed time saves.
  5. Maintenance if you like it. Some people move from nightly use to 2 to 3 nights a week instead of stopping completely. That is a personal choice, not a requirement.

Good reasons to stop or pause

  • Pregnancy, trying to conceive or breastfeeding. Stop retinol creams, including the GlutaNex Night Cream, unless your doctor says it is fine. Read glutathione in pregnancy.
  • Irritation that does not settle. Burning, swelling or a rash: stop and see a doctor if it lasts. Mild tingling at first is different; see glutathione cream burning.
  • Before waxing, threading, peels or laser. Pause retinol and acid creams for a few days before and after, and tell your dermatologist what you use.
  • Before starting a prescription cream. Ask your dermatologist whether to keep both.

You do not need to taper a cosmetic cream. You can simply stop.

Switching to another product instead of stopping

Some people stop one glutathione cream only to start another the same week. That is fine, with a few precautions:

  • Check the new label. If the new product also has retinol, AHAs, BHAs or other strong actives, do not use it on the same night as anything similar.
  • Restart slowly. Even if your skin was used to the old cream, a new formula can behave differently. Begin at 2 to 3 nights a week and patch test first.
  • Do not expect a reset. Switching does not "restart" results or speed them up. Tone changes build gradually over weeks with any product.
  • Avoid unlabelled "stronger" creams. If you are switching because results felt slow, be careful of products that promise faster change; some contain steroids or other banned substances. See glutathione side effects for warning signs.

Whatever you switch to, the morning routine stays the same: gentle cleanse, moisturiser if needed, sunscreen. That is the part that protects whatever improvement you have already seen, and it costs far less than any cream.

Seasonal breaks are also common. Some people use a glutathione night cream through the harsh summer months and pause in a cold, dry winter if their skin feels sensitive, or the other way round. Either works, as long as sunscreen continues all year: winter sun in north India and the hills still tans skin. See glutathione cream in winter.

Stopping because you saw no change?

Check the basics first: was it used for at least 8 to 12 weeks, with sunscreen every single morning? Most "it did not work" stories are a few weeks of use without sun protection. If you did all that and see no change, patchy pigmentation on both cheeks may be melasma, which needs a dermatologist. Realistic timelines are in how long glutathione takes to work.
