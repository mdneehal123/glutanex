Glutathione cream with olive oil: can you use both?

Can you use olive oil with glutathione cream? Yes, on dry skin, as a few drops pressed over the cream or on body patches, never mixed into the jar. Right order, who should skip it, winter tips and honest results.

Can I use olive oil with glutathione cream?

Yes, if your skin is dry. Apply the glutathione cream first on clean skin, wait a few minutes, then press one or two drops of olive oil over dry patches only. Never mix the oil into the jar. If you have oily or acne-prone skin, skip olive oil on the face because it can feel heavy and clog pores.

Should I apply olive oil before or after glutathione cream?

After. Oil forms a seal on top of the skin, so putting it on first can stop the cream from spreading evenly. Cream first as a thin layer, then a drop of oil on top only where the skin feels tight or flaky.

Does olive oil with glutathione cream make skin fair?

No. Olive oil can make dry skin feel softer and look less dull, and a glutathione cream used with daily sunscreen may help tan and dark marks look lighter slowly over 8 to 12 weeks. Neither changes your natural skin colour.

The right way to use both

  1. Patch test the oil on your jawline for two or three nights before using it on the whole face. Look for bumps, itching or redness.
  2. Wash your face with a gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash and pat it almost dry.
  3. Apply the glutathione cream as a thin layer, about a pea-sized amount for the whole face.
  4. Wait 5 to 10 minutes so the cream settles.
  5. Warm one or two drops of olive oil between your fingertips and press them onto dry areas only: cheeks, around the nose, chin. Do not rub hard.
  6. Keep the oil away from the eyes and any pimples.
  7. Sunscreen every morning, SPF 30 or higher, after washing off the night layers.

Which olive oil, and how much

For skin, a plain extra virgin or cold-pressed olive oil without added fragrance is the simplest choice. Cooking blends, flavoured oils and "pomace" oil are meant for food, not the face. Keep a small separate bottle for skin so you are not dipping greasy fingers into the kitchen bottle, and close it tightly; oil that smells sharp or stale has gone off and should not go on your face. One or two drops is enough for the face. More than that just sits on top, feels sticky on the pillow and gathers dust if you step out.

Who should skip olive oil on the face

  • Oily or acne-prone skin. Olive oil is heavy, and some people find it leads to small bumps or pimples. A light, oil-free moisturiser is a better partner; see glutathione for oily skin and can glutathione cream cause pimples.
  • Humid weather. In Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata or the monsoon months anywhere, an extra oil layer often feels greasy. Keep it for dry winter nights.
  • Very sensitive or eczema-prone skin. Plant oils do not suit everyone with a weak skin barrier. Ask a dermatologist what to use.
  • Irritated or broken skin, including skin that is red after a new active cream.

Olive oil for tan and dullness: an honest view

Olive oil can make dry, flaky skin look smoother and less dull, which is a fair reason to use it in winter. It does not remove tan or fade dark spots. Tan and marks look lighter slowly, over weeks, with shade, covering up and daily sunscreen; read glutathione cream in winter and glutathione cream for dry skin.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if your skin breaks into many small bumps, itches or turns red after using oil, if dryness cracks or bleeds, or if dark patches keep spreading on the cheeks (this can be melasma, which needs a doctor; see glutathione for melasma).
