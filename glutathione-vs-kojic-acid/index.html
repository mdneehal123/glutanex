Glutathione vs kojic acid: which is better for your skin?

How the two brightening ingredients compare, and whether to combine them.

By the GlutaNex team · Updated 11 October 2026 · 2 sources cited

Key takeaways
  • Both slow melanin production; glutathione is usually gentler and adds antioxidant protection.
  • Kojic acid may help stubborn spots but irritates some skin.
  • They can be combined; introduce one product at a time.

Glutathione and kojic acid are both used in products for tan, dark spots and uneven-looking tone. They work in related ways but feel different on the skin.

GlutathioneKojic acid
What it isAntioxidant tripeptide the body makes naturallyCompound produced by certain fungi, often from fermented rice
How it worksSlows tyrosinase, shifts melanin to lighter forms, antioxidantSlows tyrosinase by binding the copper it needs
GentlenessGenerally gentleCan irritate or cause allergic reactions in some people
Extra benefitsAntioxidant protection, recycles vitamins C and EMild antibacterial properties
Best forOverall dullness and uneven tone, sensitive skinStubborn spots, in people who tolerate it

Which is better?

Neither is "stronger" in every way. Glutathione is usually the gentler choice for everyday use and adds antioxidant protection. Kojic acid may suit stubborn spots but is more likely to irritate. Evidence for both comes mostly from small studies (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016, Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025).

Can you use them together?

Yes, many products combine them. If you use separate products, introduce one at a time, patch test, and avoid layering several strong actives on the same night.

Whatever you choose

Sunscreen every morning matters more than any single ingredient. Without it, new tan and spots keep forming.
