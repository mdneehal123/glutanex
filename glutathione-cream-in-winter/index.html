Glutathione cream in winter: a routine for dry skin

How to use a glutathione cream in cold, dry weather without flaking, and why sunscreen still matters.

By the GlutaNex team · Updated 25 October 2026 · 1 sources cited

Key takeaways
  • Winter is a good time to start: milder sun lets existing tan fade more easily.
  • For dry skin, apply on dry skin, add a moisturiser on top and use retinol creams every other night if you flake.
  • Keep SPF 30+ sunscreen every morning; UVA stays strong in Indian winters.

Can I use glutathione cream in winter?

Yes. Winter is a good time to start a glutathione cream: the sun is milder, so tan fades more easily. Apply it at night on clean skin, add a moisturiser on top if your skin feels dry, and keep wearing SPF 30+ sunscreen every morning.

Do I need sunscreen in winter?

Yes. UVA rays, which drive tan and dark spots, stay strong through Indian winters and pass through haze, clouds and window glass. Use a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher with a good PA rating every morning, even on cool or foggy days.

Why winter is a good season to start

Milder sun means less new tan, so the tan you already have can fade with less fighting back. Starting now also means your skin has had 8–12 weeks of steady use by the end of winter, which is how long glutathione creams usually take to show a brighter, more even look.

Adjust for dry skin

  • Cleanse gently. Lukewarm water, not hot. If your skin feels tight, use Gluta Boost Face Wash once a day at night and just rinse with water in the morning.
  • Apply on dry skin. Wait a minute after washing; actives on damp skin can sting more in cold weather.
  • Layer a moisturiser. Apply the night cream first, wait a minute, then a plain moisturiser if you need more comfort.
  • Go slower with retinol. GlutaNex Glutathione Night Cream contains retinol and salicylic acid, which can feel stronger in dry air. Use it every other night if you see flaking, and build up again.

A simple winter routine

WhenSteps
MorningRinse or gentle cleanse, moisturiser, SPF 30+ sunscreen
NightCleanse, pat dry, pea-sized amount of night cream, moisturiser if dry
WeeklySkip scrubs; if skin flakes, take one or two nights off actives

What to avoid in winter

Very hot showers on the face, harsh scrubs, alcohol-heavy toners and stacking several new actives at once. They strip the skin barrier, and a damaged barrier makes dullness and uneven tone look worse.
