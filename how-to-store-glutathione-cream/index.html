How to store glutathione cream: heat, light, expiry and signs it has gone off

How should I store glutathione cream?

Keep the lid tightly closed and store the jar in a cool, dry, dark place, such as a bedroom drawer or cupboard, away from sunlight, the stove and a steamy bathroom. Scoop with clean, dry fingers or a spatula, and never let water get into the jar.

Does glutathione cream expire?

Yes. Every cream has a use-before or expiry date printed on the pack, and many also show a PAO symbol, an open jar with a number such as 6M or 12M, telling you how many months it stays good after opening. Follow whichever date comes first, and do not use it after.

Why has my glutathione cream changed colour?

A slow, slight change can happen because glutathione and vitamin C react with air, light and heat. A clear darkening to yellow-brown, a strong new smell, separation, a watery or grainy texture, or any spots of mould mean the cream has gone off. Stop using it.

Why storage matters for this kind of cream

Glutathione and vitamin C are both antioxidants: their job is to react with oxygen and free radicals. That also makes them sensitive in the jar. Air, light and heat slowly use them up before they reach your skin. Retinol, also found in many night creams, breaks down in light and heat too. Good storage will not make a cream stronger, but poor storage can make it weaker well before the printed date. Read more on stability in reduced vs oxidised glutathione.

Daily habits that keep the cream fresh

  1. Close the lid straight after use. Every minute open lets air in.
  2. Use clean, dry fingers or a small spatula. Wet fingers bring water and germs into the jar, which is how mould starts.
  3. Take only what you need, about a pea-sized amount for the face, and never put extra back in the jar.
  4. Do not add water, oils or other creams into the jar to "make it last".
  5. Keep it in its original jar and box. Decanting into another container exposes it to air and you lose the batch and date details.
  6. Wipe the rim with a clean tissue so the lid seals properly.

How to read expiry and PAO symbols

  • Use-before or expiry date: the last date the sealed product is expected to stay good. Look on the jar base, the box or the crimp label.
  • PAO (period after opening): a small open-jar symbol with a number and "M", such as 6M or 12M, meaning months after you first open it.
  • Batch number: a code that identifies when the product was made. Note it if you ever need to contact the brand.

Season-by-season checklist

SeasonWhat to watch
Summer (April to June)Heat is the main risk. Keep the jar in the coolest room, away from windows and the kitchen. Never leave it in a parked car or in a bag on a two-wheeler.
MonsoonHumidity and damp fingers invite mould. Store it outside the bathroom, dry your hands well, and check the surface of the cream for spots every week or so.
WinterUsually the easiest season. Cold can make a cream slightly firmer; that is normal. Avoid keeping it right next to a room heater.

If you live by the coast or in a very humid city, a small airtight box in a drawer adds protection. If a jar has been left somewhere hot for hours, check its smell, colour and texture against the table above before using it again.

Travel and gym bags

  • Keep the jar in a zip pouch inside your bag, not an outer pocket in the sun.
  • On flights, a 30 g jar fits cabin liquid rules in most cases; keep it in your clear bag.
  • Do not leave it in a parked car.
  • For short trips, take the face wash and sunscreen and skip actives if you are unsure about storage. A missed night or two does not undo weeks of routine.
