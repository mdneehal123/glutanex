Glutathione cream with vitamin C serum: how to layer them

Morning or night, which goes first, and why a vitamin C serum is usually best kept for the morning when your night cream has retinol and salicylic acid.

Can I use glutathione cream with vitamin C serum?

Yes. Using glutathione cream with vitamin C serum works well if you split them: vitamin C serum in the morning under sunscreen, glutathione cream at night. If your night cream also contains retinol or salicylic acid, avoid putting a strong vitamin C serum under it on the same night, as stacking acids can irritate.

Should vitamin C serum go before or after glutathione cream?

Thinner products go first. If you ever use both in one routine, apply the vitamin C serum on clean, dry skin, wait a minute or two, then the cream. But for most people the easier and gentler option is vitamin C in the morning and the glutathione cream at night.

Why the morning is usually best for vitamin C serum

Vitamin C is an antioxidant. Its most useful job is helping skin cope with daytime stress from sun and pollution, so it does the most good under your sunscreen, not on a pillow. Glutathione creams, on the other hand, are often made for night use. That gives you a natural split:

  • Morning: vitamin C serum, then moisturiser if needed, then sunscreen.
  • Night: glutathione cream on clean skin.

For a side-by-side look at the two ingredients themselves, read glutathione vs vitamin C. This page is only about layering them as products.

Do you even need a separate vitamin C serum?

Not necessarily. GlutaNex Glutathione Night Cream already contains vitamin C, along with glutathione and niacinamide. If you use it at night and a good sunscreen in the morning, you are already getting vitamin C in your routine. A separate serum is optional, not a requirement. It makes most sense if:

  • you want an antioxidant layer under your sunscreen on hot, sunny days or a long commute;
  • your skin already tolerates vitamin C serums well;
  • you are happy to keep a second product fresh (see storage below).

If your budget is tight, sunscreen gives far more value than a second serum.

Why not use both at night?

Many vitamin C serums, especially those with pure ascorbic acid, are acidic. GlutaNex Night Cream also contains retinol and salicylic acid (a BHA). Putting an acidic serum under a cream with retinol and BHA means three strong actives on the same skin, on the same night. For many people that leads to:

  • stinging or a warm, burning feeling;
  • redness and tightness the next morning;
  • dry, flaky patches, especially around the nose and mouth.

If you get any of these, stop the serum first, not the sunscreen. Our guide to glutathione cream burning explains what is normal and when to stop completely.

Some gentler vitamin C forms are less likely to sting, but the simple rule still holds: one active-heavy routine per time of day is easier on skin than stacking everything at night.

If you still want both in one routine

  1. Patch test each product separately for 24 hours first.
  2. Introduce one at a time. Get used to the night cream for 2–3 weeks (2–3 nights a week, then more) before adding a serum.
  3. Use the serum on non-cream nights only, if you want it at night at all. On the nights you use the glutathione cream, skip it.
  4. Keep it thin. A few drops of serum and a pea-sized amount of cream cover the whole face.
  5. Back off at the first sign of irritation. Go back to the simple AM/PM split.

Adjusting the routine by skin type and season

  • Oily or acne-prone skin: choose a light, water-based vitamin C serum and a gel sunscreen. In humid months you may not need a moisturiser in the morning at all; the serum and sunscreen are enough.
  • Dry skin: put a moisturiser between the vitamin C serum and sunscreen, and on night-cream nights add a plain moisturiser on top if you feel tight. In northern winters, use the night cream every other night until any flaking settles.
  • Sensitive skin: start with the night cream alone for a month. Add vitamin C later, a few mornings a week, and pick a gentler, fragrance-free formula. See sensitive skin.
  • Summer and long commutes: this is when the morning vitamin C layer is most useful, but only alongside generous sunscreen, reapplied when you are outdoors for long. Tan from one unprotected day can undo weeks of care.
  • Shaving or threading days: skip the vitamin C serum on freshly shaved or threaded skin for a day, as it can sting.

Whatever your skin type, change one thing at a time. If you add the serum and the night cream in the same week and your skin reacts, you will not know which one caused it.

Keeping a vitamin C serum fresh in Indian heat

Vitamin C serums oxidise with heat, light and air. A serum that has turned dark orange or brown has lost much of its strength and may irritate more. To make it last:

  • Close the cap tightly after every use and keep it in the box, away from sunlight and the bathroom geyser.
  • Buy a small bottle you will finish in a couple of months.
  • Do not keep it in a hot car or handbag through summer.

Creams also need care in heat; see how to store glutathione cream.

What to expect

Neither product works overnight. A 2025 systematic review of topical glutathione described its effects as modest (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025), and vitamin C also works gradually. Give any routine 8–12 weeks with daily sunscreen before judging; how long glutathione cream takes to work sets out what to look for week by week. Neither ingredient changes your natural skin colour; they help tan and dullness look lighter and more even over time.
