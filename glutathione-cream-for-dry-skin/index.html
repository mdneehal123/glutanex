Glutathione cream for dry skin: how to use it without flaking

Why some glutathione creams feel drying, how to buffer retinol and BHA, and a simple routine for dry Indian skin.

Is glutathione cream good for dry skin?

It can be, if you use it carefully. Glutathione itself is not drying, but many glutathione night creams also contain retinol or salicylic acid, which can cause flaking on dry skin. Start 2 to 3 nights a week, buffer it with a plain moisturiser, and wear sunscreen every morning.

Why does my glutathione cream make my skin dry?

Usually because of the other actives in the formula, not the glutathione. Retinol speeds up skin turnover and salicylic acid exfoliates inside pores, and both can leave dry skin tight or flaky in the first weeks. Using it less often and adding a moisturiser usually settles this.

Can I use moisturiser with glutathione cream?

Yes. On dry skin a moisturiser helps the cream feel comfortable. Apply the glutathione cream on clean, dry skin, wait about a minute, then smooth a plain, fragrance-free moisturiser on top. If you still flake, try the moisturiser sandwich: moisturiser, cream, then moisturiser again.

What makes a glutathione cream feel drying

Glutathione is an antioxidant and does not dry the skin by itself. In the best-known topical study, a 2% glutathione lotion actually improved skin moisture and smoothness over 10 weeks (Watanabe et al., 2014). When a glutathione cream feels drying, the reason is almost always the company it keeps:

  • Retinol (vitamin A) speeds up how quickly skin renews itself. In the first two to four weeks this can show up as tightness, fine flaking around the nose and mouth, or mild stinging.
  • Salicylic acid (BHA) exfoliates inside pores. Great for oily skin, but on dry skin it can feel like too much if used every night from the start.
  • Alcohol-heavy bases and fragrance in some budget creams also strip moisture.
  • Your own routine: hot water, foaming face washes used twice a day, scrubs and toners add up.

Buffering: the moisturiser sandwich

Buffering means placing a moisturiser between your skin and an active so that less of the active reaches the skin at once. It slightly softens the effect, but it also makes it far easier to keep using the cream, and steady use is what matters over weeks.

  1. Wash with lukewarm water and a gentle cleanser. Pat dry and wait a minute or two; actives on damp skin sting more.
  2. If your skin is very dry, apply a thin layer of a plain, fragrance-free moisturiser first.
  3. Smooth a pea-sized amount of the night cream over the face and neck, avoiding the eyes, the corners of the nose and the lips.
  4. Wait a minute, then add another layer of moisturiser on top.
  5. On dry patches around the mouth or nose, a dab of plain petroleum jelly at the end helps.

What to look for in the moisturiser you pair with it: a cream (not a gel) texture, ingredients such as glycerin, ceramides, shea butter or squalane, and no added fragrance or alcohol near the top of the list. It does not need to be expensive; a plain pharmacy moisturiser in a tube or tub works well. Avoid layering a second brightening or exfoliating product on the same night, because that brings back the dryness you are trying to avoid.

Most people with normal-to-dry skin only need the moisturiser on top. Use the full sandwich on very dry skin, in winter, or when flaking has started.

Winter, AC and travel

North Indian winters, hill stations and long hours in air-conditioned offices or trains all pull water out of the skin. In these conditions:

  • Drop one night a week of the active cream and add a richer moisturiser.
  • Avoid very hot showers on the face, even when it is cold.
  • Keep a small tube of plain moisturiser at your desk or in your bag for midday tightness.
  • A room humidifier or a bowl of water near the bed helps some people in dry Delhi or Punjab winters.

The morning matters just as much

Retinol and acids make skin more sensitive to the sun, and sun is the main driver of tan and dark spots in India. Every morning: rinse or gently cleanse, apply moisturiser, then a broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen. Cream or lotion sunscreens usually feel better on dry skin than gels or mattifying formulas. Skipping sunscreen while using a night cream with retinol works against the very tan you are trying to lighten.

Cleansing for dry skin

Wash once properly at night and just rinse with water in the morning if your skin feels tight. A mild face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash at night is enough; there is no need for a second cleanse, scrubs or peel-off masks. Normal settling-in vs a reaction

Mild tightness, light flaking and a brief tingle in the first two weeks are common with retinol and usually settle. Stop the cream and use only a gentle moisturiser if you get burning that lasts, redness that spreads, swelling, itching or small bumps. If it does not calm down within a few days, see a doctor. What to expect

Studies on glutathione creams are small and show modest changes (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025). With steady use and daily sunscreen, skin may look brighter and more even-looking over 8 to 12 weeks, and tan may look lighter. Dry skin that is well moisturised also simply looks more radiant, because a smooth surface reflects light better. Natural skin colour does not change, and no cream works in a few days.

A realistic way to judge progress: take a photo in the same daylight spot once a month, without filters. Look for smoother texture, fewer flaky patches and a more even-looking tone rather than a change in shade. If after three months your skin feels comfortable but nothing looks different, that is useful information too; read how long glutathione takes to work before deciding what to change.
