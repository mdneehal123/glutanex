Glutathione cream vs tablets vs injections

The honest differences, including the safety warnings.

By the GlutaNex team · Updated 12 October 2026 · 5 sources cited

Key takeaways
  • Cream: applied to skin, low risk, modest gradual results.
  • Tablets: mixed evidence, take only with medical advice.
  • Injections: no clinical trial for skin lightening and regulator warnings; avoid.

What is the best glutathione supplement for skin?

No glutathione supplement is proven best for skin. Small studies used 250 to 500 mg a day for 4 to 12 weeks and found modest, mixed changes in skin tone. Supplements are not regulated like medicines, so speak to a doctor before taking any, especially if pregnant.

For skincare, a glutathione cream is the simpler, lower-risk option. Studies: Arjinpathana & Asawanonda, 2012; Weschawalit et al., 2017.

What is liposomal glutathione?

Liposomal glutathione is glutathione wrapped in tiny fat-like bubbles called liposomes, designed to protect it from digestion and improve absorption. It is usually more expensive, and evidence that it gives better skin results than ordinary glutathione is still limited.

Ask a doctor before starting any supplement.

What is the recommended dose of glutathione for skin whitening?

There is no officially recommended dose of glutathione for skin whitening. Published studies used 250 to 500 mg by mouth daily for 4 to 12 weeks, with only modest changes in skin tone. It does not permanently whiten skin. Ask a doctor before taking supplements, and avoid injections.

Regulators have warned against skin-lightening injections (US FDA warning, 2015). See glutathione and skin whitening: what is true.

What is L-glutathione?

L-glutathione is simply the natural form of glutathione, the same one your body makes. The "L" refers to the form of the amino acids it is built from. Most products labelled L-glutathione or reduced glutathione contain this active form.

In creams it may appear as glutathione or oxidised glutathione (GSSG). See what is glutathione.

For everyday skincare, a glutathione cream or face wash is the simple, low-risk option. Tablets are supplements with mixed evidence, and injections carry real safety risks.

Why we recommend against injections for skin

The US FDA warning, 2015 stated that injectable skin-lightening products may be unsafe and ineffective, may contain unknown substances, and that unsafe injections can spread infection. No injectable product is approved for skin lightening.

What about tablets?

Glutathione supplements are widely sold, but evidence for visible skin changes is mixed. If you are considering them, talk to a doctor, especially if you take other medicines or are pregnant.

The simple approach

A gentle face wash, a glutathione cream at night and sunscreen every morning. See how to use glutathione cream.

What the studies used

  • Cream: a 2% oxidised glutathione lotion for 10 weeks (Watanabe et al., 2014).
  • Tablets: 500 mg a day for 4 weeks (Arjinpathana & Asawanonda, 2012), and 250 mg a day for 12 weeks (Weschawalit et al., 2017).
  • Injections: no clinical trial for skin lightening (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016).

Cost and convenience

Creams are the most affordable and simplest to use every day. Injections are expensive, need repeated visits, and cheap versions may be counterfeit.
