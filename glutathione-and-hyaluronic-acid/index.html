Glutathione and hyaluronic acid together: what each does and how to layer

Why glutathione and hyaluronic acid pair easily, how to use a hyaluronic acid serum on damp skin, and how to add it under a night cream on dry nights.

Can I use glutathione and hyaluronic acid together?

Yes. Glutathione and hyaluronic acid together are an easy pair with no known conflict: glutathione is an antioxidant used for a brighter, more even look, while hyaluronic acid simply holds water in the skin. Many creams already contain both. If you add a separate hyaluronic acid serum, apply it first, then the cream.

Should hyaluronic acid go before or after glutathione cream?

Before. Hyaluronic acid serums are thin and water-based, so apply them to clean, slightly damp skin, let them sink in for a minute or two, then apply the glutathione cream on top. The cream helps seal the water in, so skin stays comfortable overnight.

Hyaluronic acid has nothing to do with pigment. Its value next to glutathione is comfort: well-hydrated skin looks smoother and reflects light better, so the overall look is fresher. A 2025 systematic review found topical glutathione's own effects modest (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025), so a comfortable, hydrated base matters.

Using glutathione and hyaluronic acid together on dry nights

On nights when your skin feels tight (AC rooms, winter in the north, after a long flight, or while you are getting used to retinol), a plain hyaluronic acid serum under the night cream can help. Here is the order:

  1. Cleanse gently with lukewarm water and a mild face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash.
  2. Leave the skin slightly damp. Pat with a towel so it is not dripping.
  3. Apply 2–3 drops of hyaluronic acid serum and press it in with your palms.
  4. Wait 1–2 minutes, until the skin no longer feels wet. Retinol creams can sting more on wet skin, so do not rush this.
  5. Apply a pea-sized amount of the glutathione cream over the face and neck, avoiding eyes and lips.
  6. Add a plain moisturiser on top if you are still dry.

Why damp skin matters

Hyaluronic acid works by holding water. On damp skin it has water to hold right away. In very dry air, if applied on bone-dry skin and left without a cream on top, it can leave skin feeling tight. The fix is simple: damp skin first, a cream on top to seal it. In humid coastal weather (Mumbai, Chennai, Kerala, Kolkata in the monsoon) hyaluronic acid feels light and is rarely a problem.

Any conflicts?

No. Hyaluronic acid is one of the gentlest skincare ingredients and pairs well with glutathione, niacinamide, vitamin C, retinol and salicylic acid. It is often used to make strong actives more comfortable. A few practical cautions:

  • Do not use it as an excuse to add more actives. A hyaluronic acid serum is fine; a hyaluronic serum that also contains AHAs or high-strength acids is a different product. Check the label.
  • Fragrance: if your skin is reactive, choose a fragrance-free serum. See sensitive skin.
  • Patch test any new serum for 24 hours, as with any product.

Morning use

Hyaluronic acid is fine in the morning too: a few drops on damp skin, a light moisturiser if needed, then sunscreen. The glutathione night cream stays at night only. If you also use a vitamin C serum in the morning, apply vitamin C first, then hyaluronic acid, then sunscreen; see glutathione cream with vitamin C serum.

If you see redness, peeling or a burning feeling that lasts, go back to the night cream alone, 2–3 nights a week, and read glutathione cream burning. Hyaluronic acid is very unlikely to be the cause; the stronger actives usually are.

Who benefits most from adding a serum

  • People in their first weeks of a retinol night cream, when mild dryness is common.
  • Dry or mature skin; see glutathione cream for 40 plus.
  • Anyone in dry air: northern winters, desert cities or offices with strong AC.

Oily skin in humid weather usually does not need an extra layer; the hyaluronic acid already in the cream is enough. If your T-zone is oily but your cheeks are dry, apply the serum only on the cheeks; see combination skin.

On budget: a plain hyaluronic acid serum does not need to be expensive. Price mostly reflects packaging and branding, not how well it holds water. A simple, fragrance-free serum in a small bottle that you finish within a few months is a sensible choice. Spend first on daily sunscreen, which does far more for tan and an even-looking tone than any hydrating serum. And remember that neither hyaluronic acid nor glutathione changes your natural skin colour: they help skin look healthier, smoother and more even over time.
