What are the key facts about glutathione for skin?

Glutathione is an antioxidant the body makes from three amino acids. Small trials found modest, gradual drops in skin melanin: a 2% lotion over 10 weeks and 250–500 mg tablets over 4–12 weeks. It does not change natural skin colour, and injections for skin lightening lack trials and carry regulator warnings.

The molecule

What it isA tripeptide antioxidant made of glutamic acid (glutamate), cysteine and glycine; often written GSH
Where it is madeIn the body's own cells, mostly the liver
Main jobProtects cells from oxidative damage and recycles vitamins C and E
Link to skin toneSlows tyrosinase, the enzyme that makes melanin, and may shift production towards lighter pheomelanin (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016)

What the reviews conclude

  • A 2025 systematic review of topical glutathione (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025): promising, but studies are few, small and short.
  • The Indian dermatology review (Sonthalia, Daulatabad & Sarkar, IJDVL 2016): short-term evidence is encouraging for creams and tablets, not strong enough to call glutathione a treatment for melasma, and no trial supports IV glutathione for skin lightening.

Timelines and numbers to remember

Fresher lookOften 4–8 weeks of nightly use
Lighter-looking tanUsually 8–12 weeks, with daily SPF 30+ sunscreen
Doses studied (tablets)250–500 mg a day, for 4–12 weeks
Cream toleranceIn the 10-week lotion study, 1 of 30 women had mild redness that cleared in two days
InjectionsNo clinical trial for skin lightening; warned against by regulators (US FDA warning, 2015)

What glutathione does not do

It does not change natural skin colour, results fade without sun protection, and no cream, tablet or injection is proven to whiten skin permanently.
