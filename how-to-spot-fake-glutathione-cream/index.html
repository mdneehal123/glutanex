How do I know if a glutathione cream is fake?

Check the seller, the full ingredient list, batch number and expiry date, a printed company address and a realistic price. Be careful of creams that promise fairness in days, have no ingredient list, or are sold only by anonymous sellers.

Can a fake glutathione cream harm skin?

Yes. Some unregulated creams contain strong ingredients that are not on the label and can cause burns, thinning or patchy colour. Stop using any cream that burns or changes your skin and see a doctor.

What should a genuine label show?

Brand and company name with address, the ingredient list, net weight, batch number, manufacturing and expiry dates, and a customer contact number.

To spot a fake glutathione cream, check eight things: who sells it, whether the full ingredient list is printed, the batch number and expiry date, a real company address, a believable price, the claims on the pack, the packaging quality and what customers say after the parcel arrives. Any cream that promises fair skin in a few days should be treated as a warning sign.

Fake and unregulated skin creams are a real problem in India, and some contain ingredients that are not on the label. This guide helps you check before you put anything on your face.

Why a cream that works too fast is a red flag

Skin renews over about 4 to 6 weeks, and a glutathione cream used with daily sunscreen may help tan and dark marks look lighter slowly over 8 to 12 weeks. A cream that changes your colour within a week may contain a strong hidden ingredient. See glutathione cream results and glutathione for skin whitening.

If you have already used one

  • Stop using it if it burns, itches, or your skin turns red, thin or patchy.
  • Wash your face with a gentle cleanser and use a plain moisturiser.
  • See a dermatologist if the reaction does not settle, and take the jar with you.

Read glutathione cream burning for what to do when a cream stings.

What a suspicious online listing looks like

  • Photos copied from other brands, with a different jar in the box.
  • Reviews that are all the same length and posted on the same day.
  • A price far below the brand's own site, with "limited stock" pressure.
  • A message asking you to pay by UPI to a personal number.
  • No way to contact the seller after you order.

What to do before you order

  1. Open the brand's own website and compare the price, jar and ingredient list.
  2. Ask the seller for a photo of the label showing batch and expiry.
  3. Choose Cash on Delivery or a platform with a return option.
  4. Open the parcel on arrival and check that the seal is intact.
