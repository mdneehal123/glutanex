Gua sha with glutathione cream: can you use them together?

Can you do gua sha with glutathione cream? Not with a retinol or salicylic acid cream on your face. Use a plain moisturiser or a few drops of face oil for gua sha, and keep the active cream for other nights. Steps, pressure, cleaning the stone and honest results.

Can I use glutathione cream for gua sha?

It is better not to if the cream has retinol or salicylic acid, as GlutaNex Night Cream does. Dragging a stone over an active cream spreads it unevenly and adds friction, which raises the chance of stinging. Use a plain moisturiser or a few drops of face oil for gua sha instead.

Should I do gua sha before or after night cream?

Before, and with a plain moisturiser or oil. Do gua sha first, gently wipe off any extra oil, wait a few minutes, then apply a thin layer of night cream with your fingertips. In the first weeks of retinol, keep gua sha for nights you skip the retinol cream.

Does gua sha make your face slimmer?

No. Gua sha can make the face look less puffy for a few hours and feels relaxing, but it does not slim the face, lift skin or reduce fat. Any change you see the next morning is temporary.

You can use gua sha and glutathione cream in the same routine, but do not use a retinol or salicylic acid cream as the slip for the stone. Do gua sha with a plain moisturiser or a few drops of face oil, then apply a thin layer of glutathione cream with your fingertips, or keep the two on different nights.

Gua sha stones and face rollers are now common in India, sold as "face sculpting" tools. They can be a relaxing part of a night routine, but they work best with gentle pressure and a plain base, and they do not replace sunscreen or a steady routine.

How to do gua sha safely, step by step

  1. Wash your face with a gentle face wash such as Gluta Boost Face Wash and pat dry.
  2. Clean the stone with mild soap and water and dry it.
  3. Apply a slip: a plain, fragrance-free moisturiser or 2 to 3 drops of a simple face oil. Enough that the stone glides, not drags.
  4. Hold the stone almost flat against the skin, at a low angle.
  5. Use light pressure and slow strokes: from the centre of the chin along the jaw to the ear, from the side of the nose across the cheek, and from the eyebrows up to the hairline. 3 to 5 strokes per area.
  6. Keep it to 5 minutes. Skin should look slightly pink at most, never red, bruised or sore.
  7. Wipe off extra oil with a soft tissue and wait a few minutes.
  8. Apply a pea-sized amount of night cream with your fingertips if it is a cream night.
  9. Sunscreen every morning, SPF 30 or higher.

A cold stone straight from the fridge feels nice on puffy mornings; for cold tools, read ice cube with glutathione cream.

Who should skip gua sha

  • Active pimples or acne: dragging a stone over them spreads irritation and can leave marks; see glutathione for acne marks.
  • Broken, sunburnt or freshly waxed, threaded or shaved skin.
  • Sensitive skin that flushes easily or has visible small red veins; see glutathione cream for sensitive skin.
  • Recent fillers, injections or facial procedures: ask the doctor who did them first.
  • Anyone who bruises easily or takes blood-thinning medicine: ask your doctor.

Under-eye area and dark circles

Use the lightest touch under the eyes, or just a cool stone held still for a few seconds. Keep active creams away from the eyelids. Gua sha may make morning puffiness look less, but it does not change the colour of dark circles; read glutathione for dark circles for an honest view.

Keeping the stone clean

  • Wash it with mild soap and water after every use and dry it fully.
  • Store it in a clean pouch or box, not loose on a bathroom shelf.
  • Do not share it. If it chips or cracks, replace it, because rough edges can scratch.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if gua sha leaves bruises, broken veins or soreness that lasts, if pimples get worse, or if your skin stays red or itchy after a new routine.
