Glutathione for wrinkles: what the evidence says and what works better

What studies found about glutathione and wrinkles, why retinol, peptides and sunscreen do most of the work, and a simple anti-ageing routine.

Does glutathione help with wrinkles?

Evidence on glutathione for wrinkles is limited. One 12-week study of glutathione taken by mouth saw some wrinkle improvement with the reduced form and a trend towards better elasticity, but most results were not statistically significant. For the look of fine lines, retinol, peptides and daily sunscreen do the heavy lifting.

Is glutathione good for anti-ageing?

As a supporting antioxidant, yes, it can play a part. Glutathione helps protect skin from everyday oxidative stress and is used for a brighter, more even look. It does not reverse ageing or remove deep lines. Sunscreen and retinol have a much stronger track record for anti-ageing.

Glutathione for wrinkles: what the studies actually show

StudyWhat was testedWhat it found on lines and texture
Weschawalit et al., 2017Glutathione by mouth, reduced or oxidised form, 12 weeks, 60 women aged 20–50Some improvement in wrinkles with the reduced form and a trend towards better elasticity; most results were not statistically significant
Watanabe et al., 2014Leave-on glutathione lotion on the face, 10 weeks, 30 womenBetter moisture and smoothness alongside a modest fall in melanin; it was not a wrinkle study
Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025A 2025 systematic review of topical glutathionePromising for tone, but studies are few, small and short

In plain words: the only wrinkle signal comes from a supplement study, it was a trend rather than a clear result, and there is no good trial of a glutathione cream for wrinkles. Smoother, better-hydrated skin can make fine lines look softer, but that is not the same as reducing wrinkles. Whether to take glutathione supplements at all is a question for your doctor.

Why wrinkles form

  • Sun: UV breaks down collagen and elastin. Most visible skin ageing on the face comes from years of sun, not birthdays.
  • Natural ageing: collagen production slows from your mid-20s, and skin becomes thinner and drier.
  • Expression lines: smiling, frowning and squinting fold the skin in the same places thousands of times.
  • Smoking, poor sleep and pollution add oxidative stress.
  • Dryness: dehydrated skin shows fine lines more, and they soften once skin is moisturised.

Glutathione's antioxidant role fits the oxidative stress part of this list. The bigger levers, sun and collagen, are handled better by other ingredients.

A realistic anti-ageing routine

  1. Morning: gentle cleanse, moisturiser, broad-spectrum sunscreen on face, neck and the backs of the hands. Reapply if outdoors. See sunscreen guide.
  2. Night: cleanse with a mild face wash, pat dry, then a pea-sized amount of a retinol night cream. Because the GlutaNex Night Cream also contains salicylic acid, use it at night only.
  3. Weeks 1–2: 2 to 3 nights a week, plain moisturiser on other nights.
  4. Weeks 3–8: increase slowly if skin is comfortable; alternate nights suit many people.
  5. Judge at 3 months, not 3 weeks. Retinol changes in texture build slowly.

Keep retinol creams off the eyelids and lips, and use a separate gentle eye cream if you want one.

Habits that matter as much as creams

  • Shade and sunglasses: squinting in strong sun deepens crow's feet.
  • Not smoking: smoking is one of the clearest causes of early lines.
  • Sleep: regular sleep helps skin look rested; sleeping on one side every night can leave creases.
  • Food and water: a balanced diet with fruit, vegetables and protein supports skin. See glutathione-rich foods.

Across all ages the order of importance stays the same: sunscreen first, then retinol, then supporting ingredients. If your budget allows only one purchase, make it a sunscreen you like enough to wear every single day.

Indian skin note: medium and deeper skin tones often show ageing first as uneven tone and dark spots rather than deep wrinkles, and are more prone to marks after irritation. That makes a slow start with retinol, and avoiding harsh peels at home, especially important. A cream that also includes ingredients for tone, such as glutathione and niacinamide, can be a practical fit. More on tone: glutathione for pigmentation.

What creams cannot do

No cream, glutathione or otherwise, removes deep wrinkles or folds. Be wary of anything that promises to erase lines in days. Deep lines, sagging and expression lines are where a dermatologist can discuss in-clinic options. Ask them, and tell them about any retinol cream you use, before peels, laser or injections.

Also be careful with the phrase "anti-ageing glutathione" on supplements, drips and injections. Glutathione injections and IV drips lack trials for cosmetic use and carry safety warnings, and they are not an anti-ageing shortcut. If you are considering any glutathione product taken by mouth or by injection, speak to a doctor first. Compare forms in cream vs tablets vs injections.

The honest bottom line: glutathione is a reasonable supporting ingredient in a night cream, mainly for a brighter, more even look. For wrinkles themselves, put your money and patience into sunscreen and retinol first.
