Reduced vs oxidised glutathione (GSH vs GSSG): which is better for skin?

What reduced and oxidised glutathione are, why creams use one or the other, and what to check on the label.

By the GlutaNex team · Updated 14 October 2026 · 3 sources cited

Key takeaways
  • Reduced glutathione (GSH) is the active form; oxidised (GSSG) is two joined molecules the body recycles.
  • GSSG is more stable in creams and was used in the best-known lotion study.
  • Packaging, the full formula and sunscreen matter more than the form.

What is the difference between reduced and oxidised glutathione?

Reduced glutathione (GSH) is the active antioxidant form. After it neutralises a free radical, two molecules join into oxidised glutathione (GSSG). The body turns GSSG back into GSH with an enzyme, so the two forms cycle.

Is oxidised glutathione better for skin creams?

Oxidised glutathione is more stable in water-based formulas, and the best-known cream study used it. There is no head-to-head study showing one form works better in a cream.

What the studies used

The 10-week lotion study used oxidised glutathione and found a lower melanin index than placebo (Watanabe et al., 2014). A 12-week tablet study compared both forms; both trended towards lower melanin, and the reduced form also showed some wrinkle improvement (Weschawalit et al., 2017). A 2025 review found no strong reason to prefer one form in creams (Khanna et al., J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2025).

What matters more than the form

  • Packaging: an airtight, opaque jar or tube protects glutathione from air and light. Close the lid after use.
  • The whole formula: partners like niacinamide and vitamin C do much of the work.
  • Regular use and sunscreen: they decide results more than GSH vs GSSG.
  • A faint sulphur smell is normal for glutathione products.

On labels

You may see "glutathione", "reduced glutathione", "L-glutathione" or "oxidised/oxidized glutathione". All are the same molecule family; check the full ingredient list and honest claims.
