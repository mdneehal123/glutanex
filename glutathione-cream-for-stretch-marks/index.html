Does glutathione cream remove stretch marks?

No. Stretch marks are changes deep in the skin where it stretched too fast, and no cream, glutathione or otherwise, removes them. A moisturiser can make the skin feel softer and less itchy, and most marks slowly fade to a paler, flatter line on their own over months to years.

What actually helps stretch marks?

Daily moisturising and gentle massage keep the skin comfortable, and new red or dark marks usually fade most in the first year. If they bother you, a dermatologist can discuss prescription creams or in-clinic procedures that suit your skin and life stage.

Glutathione cream does not remove stretch marks, and neither does any other cream sold in India. Stretch marks form deeper in the skin, where it stretched faster than it could keep up; a good moisturiser can make them feel softer and less itchy, and new marks usually fade to a paler, flatter line on their own over months to years.

So if a product promises to "remove" or "erase" stretch marks, or to do it in a few weeks, it is overpromising. This guide explains what stretch marks are, what you can realistically do at home, and why a glutathione face cream is the wrong tool for them.

What stretch marks are

Stretch marks (doctors call them striae) are lines that appear when skin stretches quickly and the support fibres in the deeper layer break and heal unevenly. Common times are:

  • Growth spurts in the teenage years (thighs, hips, breasts, back, upper arms)
  • Pregnancy (stomach, breasts, thighs)
  • Quick weight gain or loss
  • Fast muscle gain at the gym (shoulders, chest, upper arms)
  • Long use of strong steroid creams or tablets, including steroid creams wrongly sold as "fairness" creams

They are very common and harmless. On Indian skin, new marks often look pink, purple or dark brown; older marks turn lighter than the surrounding skin and slightly sunken or wrinkly.

Can glutathione cream help?NoNo; it cannot add back colour or fill the line

Glutathione is used in skincare to help dark spots and tan look lighter over weeks. That is about pigment in the top layer of skin. Stretch marks are a change in the deeper structure, so a brightening cream does not change them. On pale old marks, trying to "even them out" with brightening products only makes the surrounding skin the target, which is not what you want.

What helps at home

  1. Moisturise daily. A plain, fragrance-free body lotion or cream after a bath keeps stretched skin supple and reduces itching. Coconut or almond oil on top is fine for dry body skin.
  2. Massage gently. Rubbing the cream in for a minute or two feels good and helps you stay regular; it does not need special tools.
  3. Do not scrub. Harsh scrubs, lemon, baking soda and home "bleach" packs irritate stretched skin and can leave darker patches.
  4. Protect from sun. Marks on arms, shoulders or the back that see sun can tan and look more obvious; use sunscreen or cover up.
  5. Be patient with new marks. Most of the fading happens on its own in the first year.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: read this first

Pregnancy is the most common reason people search for stretch mark creams. During pregnancy and breastfeeding, avoid creams with retinol or other retinoids unless your doctor says otherwise, and ask before using any new active cream on large areas of skin. A plain moisturiser on the belly, breasts and thighs is the safe everyday choice. Read glutathione in pregnancy for more, and ask your gynaecologist about anything you plan to use.

Gym stretch marks in men

Quick muscle gain often leaves marks on the shoulders, chest and inner arms. They are normal and usually fade with time. Moisturise after your shower, avoid harsh scrubs, and if you see marks appearing very fast alongside other changes, mention it to a doctor. Never use steroid creams to "lighten" skin; long use thins the skin and can cause stretch marks. More on routines for men: glutathione cream for men.

Red flags when buying a stretch mark cream

  • "Removes stretch marks permanently" or "in 7 days"
  • Before and after photos with no details
  • No full ingredient list, or a "herbal" cream with no label
  • "Whitening" or "fairness" promises for stretch marks
  • Any cream that turns out to contain a steroid such as clobetasol, especially with no prescription

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if stretch marks bother you and you want more than home care: they can discuss prescription creams for early marks and in-clinic procedures, and tell you which suit your skin type and whether you are pregnant, breastfeeding or planning a pregnancy. See a doctor soon if many wide, purple marks appear quickly with weight gain, a rounder face or high blood pressure, or if marks appeared after using a steroid cream.
